# The ECP5 in your machine

```lesson
(objective "Name the ECP5 on your board and compare how much logic each size has, and how much Zeitlos uses.")
(minutes 15)
(keywords "LFE5U (part)" "die (of a chip)" "chip database (FPGA)" "card (microSD)")
```

The ECP5 comes in sizes, named by Lattice's part number: **LFE5U**,
then the size in thousands of LUTs. These are the ones on boards that
run Zeitlos:

| Part | LUTs | Boards |
|---|---|---|
| LFE5U-12F | (see below) | Obst, Konfekt, Minze |
| LFE5U-25F | 24,288 | Lakritz, Klinge, Mozart ML0, Sergei ML0 |
| LFE5U-45F | 43,848 | Mozart ML1 and ML2, Sergei ML1 and ML2, Schoko, Noir |
| LFE5U-85F | 83,640 | the ULX3S, which comes in every size |

The 12F is a 25F inside. The 12F and 25F are made from the same
**die**, the piece of silicon in the package; Lattice sells one as the
smaller part and promises less of it.

## How much Zeitlos uses

The Zeitlos SOC is a large design. Measured for
[docs:boards](docs:boards), it took 19,060 of the 24,288 LUTs on a
Lakritz, and 21,033 of 43,848 on a Mozart ML1. (A Mozart's SOC has a
little more in it.) Features added since, such as colour, have
filled a Lakritz further.

## Room to spare

Above about three quarters full, a design gets harder to place and to
make fast. Zeitlos on a 25F is past that line; on a 45F it uses less
than half. That is why the larger boards are the comfortable ones for
adding to the gateware.

```check
(number
  (ask "How many LUTs are left free on a Mozart ML1, with Zeitlos's gateware in it?")
  (answer 22815) (from "43848 - 21033")
  (wrong-if 5228 "That is what is left on the Lakritz, 24,288 - 19,060. The Mozart ML1 has a 45F.")
  (hint "Take the LUTs used, 21,033, from the 43,848 there are."))
```

## zfpga's chip databases

To use an FPGA, a tool must know what every one of its configuration
bits does. zfpga keeps that knowledge in a **chip database**, one for
each die, read from the **card** (the machine's microSD card) in
`/data/zfpga`:

- `lfe5u25f.zdb` serves both the 25F and the 12F, since they share a
  die;
- `lfe5u45f.zdb` serves the 45F.

There is no 85F database on the card, so zfpga cannot build for an
85F there yet.

```sources
(source "docs/zfpga.md, sections 2.2 and 9.1; release/lib/mkfatimg.py, the zfpga files")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "You have a Konfekt, with its 12F. Which database does zfpga use for it?")
  (right "lfe5u25f.zdb")
  (wrong "None: there is no 12F database"
    "There is no file named for the 12F, and none is needed: the 12F is the 25F's die, so the 25F's database serves it.")
  (wrong "lfe5u45f.zdb"
    "The 45F is a different die, with a different grid of tiles.")
  (hint "The 12F and the 25F are the same silicon."))
```

So: your board's ECP5 is a 12F, 25F, 45F or 85F, and Zeitlos fills
most of a 25F and under half of a 45F.
