# Timeless applications

```lesson
(objective "Name tasks that change little over time, and say why they do not need the newest hardware.")
(minutes 12)
(keywords "timeless application (task)" "application (of a computer)")
```

You will see which tasks a computer is used for that hardly change, and
what they really need.

An **application** of a computer is a task it is used for. Many have
changed little in a century, and are likely to change little in the
next:

- reading and writing
- storing information
- communication
- mathematics and organization
- education
- controlling machines, such as a water pump
- programming

These are **timeless applications**.

```sources
(source "Timeless Computing (docs/tc.md), chapter 1, Timeless Applications")
```

```check
(choice
  (ask "Which of these is a timeless application?")
  (right "Keeping a household's accounts")
  (wrong "Playing this year's newest video game"
    "A new game is made for the newest hardware and is replaced by another next year. A timeless application is a task people did a century ago and will still do: here, organization and mathematics.")
  (wrong "Trying out the latest version of a program"
    "That task exists only because the program changed. A timeless application is a need that stays the same, such as keeping accounts.")
  (hint "Would someone a century ago have done this task, with pen and paper?"))
```

---

## What they need

None of these tasks needs the newest hardware.

A writer needs an editor that responds at once and storage that keeps
the work safe. A computer that runs a water pump needs to be small and
dependable, and to do its one task for many years.

Some people still write on computers that are decades old, or on
typewriters, because those tools serve the task and nothing else.

```check
(choice
  (ask "What does a writer need most from a computer?")
  (right "An editor that responds at once, and storage that keeps the work safe")
  (wrong "The fastest hardware there is"
    "Typing a letter asks very little of a computer: decades-old machines do it well. What matters is that the editor responds at once and the work is kept safe.")
  (wrong "A new version of the editor every year"
    "A writer needs the editor to stay out of the way. Changes to it mean learning it again, which is time not spent writing.")
  (hint "Think of what goes wrong for a writer: a slow response, or lost work."))
```

---

## Between two tools

A timeless computer sits between **pen and paper** and the **modern
desktop computer**. It can do much more than pen and paper, and it is
much simpler than a modern desktop computer. It is designed for
timeless applications in particular.

```check
(choice
  (ask "Compared with a modern desktop computer, a timeless computer is ...")
  (right "simpler, and made for timeless applications")
  (wrong "simpler, so it can do less than pen and paper"
    "It sits between the two: it does much more than pen and paper, while being much simpler than a modern desktop computer.")
  (wrong "just as complex, but older"
    "Age is not the point, simplicity is. A timeless computer is no more complex than its purpose needs.")
  (hint "It sits between pen and paper and the modern desktop computer."))
```

---

```check
(choice
  (ask "A farm needs a computer to turn its water pump on and off for the next twenty years. What matters most?")
  (right "That it is small and dependable, and keeps doing that one task")
  (wrong "That it has a large, sharp screen"
    "A pump controller is seldom looked at. What it must do is run its one task, reliably, for years.")
  (wrong "That it gets the newest software each month"
    "Every change is a chance for something to break. A controller that does one task for years is better left alone.")
  (hint "What would go wrong for the farm?"))
```

So the tasks that matter most change little, and none of them needs the
newest hardware.
